My research focuses on sleep neurobiology, hypothalamic and orexinergic circuits, NREM-dependent memory processing, and sleep dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease.

## 1. Effects of Sik3 Sleepy Mutation on Hypothalamic Function in Mice

**June 2025 - Present · Andrew Varga Lab, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai**  
**Advisor:** Dr. Andrew Varga · **Role:** Graduate Research Assistant

This ongoing project investigates how the Sik3 sleepy mutation affects sleep architecture and hypothalamic function. Beyond increased sleep duration, we are examining behavioral and physiological abnormalities, including altered feeding and sleep-state instability, to better characterize this mouse model.

My current work includes EEG/EMG analysis and chocolate-based behavioral testing to assess possible cataplexy-related abnormalities. We have observed a sleep phenotype termed DT sleep and are investigating its significance. We are also delivering a viral construct containing mutant SIK3 to the hypothalamus to investigate regional contributions to these phenotypes. I am testing curcumin as a candidate inhibitor of SIK3 activity through oral gavage, assisting with paraffin sectioning, and establishing a cryosectioning protocol for the lab. These experiments remain ongoing.

## 2. Compact Human Prepro-Orexin Promoter Optimization

**July 2024 - August 2025 · Chinese Institute for Brain Research (CIBR), Beijing**  
**Advisor:** Dr. Akihiro Yamanaka

This project addressed the AAV packaging constraint imposed by the 3.2 kb human prepro-orexin promoter. I designed truncated promoter variants through literature review and rational design, with the aim of reducing promoter size to 2.2 kb while retaining selective targeting of orexin/hypocretin neurons.

I validated promoter performance using systemic retro-orbital delivery of AAV-PHP.eB for whole-brain targeting and stereotaxic injection into the lateral hypothalamus for high-resolution axonal projection mapping. A minimal construct achieved **greater than 96% coverage and greater than 97% specificity**, supporting stable, neuron-specific transgene expression within AAV packaging constraints. I also validated expression of jGCaMP8S and compatibility with miniaturized two-photon (Mini2P)-related experiments.

**Manuscript / preprint:** **Pei, Y.**, Tesmer, A. L., Ohmura, Y., & Yamanaka, A. *Compact human prepro-orexin promoters for selective AAV2/PHP.eB-mediated targeting of orexin/hypocretin neurons.*  
**Status:** Submitted to *Cell Reports Methods*; preprint available through SSRN / Cell Press Sneak Peek (posted June 4, 2026; under review).

## 3. Neural Regulation of Eyelid Closure During Sleep

**November 2024 - June 2025 · Undergraduate thesis**  
**Thesis author:** Yuanyi Pei · **Advisor:** Dr. Akihiro Yamanaka

**Thesis title:** *Identification of the Sleep Regulatory Center Using Retrograde Tracing from the Orbicularis Oculi Muscle.*

My undergraduate thesis investigated the neural pathways linking sleep regulation to eyelid closure. I designed surgical protocols for precise injections into the orbicularis oculi muscle in mice and established a retrograde tracing pipeline using tracers such as cholera toxin subunit B (CTB).

Through tracing and immunohistochemical characterization, I identified a subpopulation of cholinergic (ChAT-positive) motor neurons in the lateral/peripheral region of the facial nucleus (7N). Preliminary evidence suggested hypothalamic inputs to these neurons. This work explored a possible link between hypothalamic sleep centers and sleep-related eyelid closure, with potential relevance to nocturnal lagophthalmos. The hypothalamic connection remains a preliminary finding.

**Status:** Undergraduate thesis completed in June 2025. No publication DOI is listed for this project.

## Additional Research Training: In Vivo Imaging and Neural Data Analysis

**June 2024 - October 2024 · Chen Liangyi Lab, Peking University, Beijing**  
**Role:** Undergraduate Research Intern

I performed stereotaxic and cranial window surgeries for in vivo two-photon and wide-field single-photon imaging in awake mice. I processed calcium imaging data with CaImAn, applying motion correction and constrained non-negative matrix factorization (CNMF) to extract neural activity traces, and used custom Python scripts to analyze large field-of-view neural dynamics during behavioral tasks.
